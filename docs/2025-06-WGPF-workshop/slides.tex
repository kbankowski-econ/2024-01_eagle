\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The EAGLE model with individual countries}
  \vspace{-1ex}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item We extend the original DSGE EAGLE model from 4 to 14 regions to better capture the heterogeneity and interdependence in the euro area. 
  \item The model embeds complementary government consumption and productive government investment, which makes it a suitable tool to analyse fiscal initiatives.
  \item This presentation focuses on trade extension, properties of main fiscal shocks, and the study of the Next Generation EU (NGEU) programme in our setup as a policy application.
  \end{itemize}
  \end{frame}

  \item Other models used at ECB and other international institutions for application of fiscal analysis, including \textcolor{royalblue}{QUEST} model from the EC, \textcolor{royalblue}{ECB-MC} and \textcolor{royalblue}{GIMF} at the IMF (Kumhof et al. (2010)).
  \end{itemize}
  \textbf{Our Contribution}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The 4 region EAGLE model does not entail the heterogeneity within EA, and so is unsuited for multiple country fiscal programmes such as the NGEU programme.
  \item We present a more granular version of the model operating in Dynare, equiped to analyse granular spill-over effects across Euro Area. 
  \end{itemize}
  \end{frame}


\section{Model specification}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Final goods in the model}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Four final goods: private consumption, private investment, government consumption, and government investment.
  \item Two intermediate goods: tradable (e.g. manufacturing) and non-tradable (e.g. services).
   \item Tradable goods can be imported and each good has a home-bias parameter. 
   \item Key contribution:  multi-country trade structure and calibration of import content, which varies throughout the four goods for each region.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Trade and net foregin asset position}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Each country may run a trade surplus or deficit. Trade imbalances between countries are reflected in their net foreign asset positions.
  \item The nominal trade balance is defined as:
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{equation*}
  TB_t \equiv \sum_{CO \neq H} S_t^{H,CO} P_{X,t}^{H,CO} \underbrace{\frac{s^{CO}}{s^H} IM_t^{CO,H}}_{\equiv X_t^{H,CO}} - \sum_{CO \neq H} P_{IM,t}^{H,CO} IM_t^{H,CO}
\end{equation*}

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $X_t^{H,CO}$: Exports from $H$ to $CO$, defined as $CO$'s imports from $H$ adjusted by the size ratio $\left(\frac{s^{CO}}{s^H}\right)$. $S_t^{H,CO}$ is the nominal exchange rate converting prices from $CO$'s to $H$'s currency.
  \item This scaling reflects how small economies can be heavily affected by trade with large partners.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Fiscal and Monetary Policy rules}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Governments are not required to balance the budget in each period with the possibility to issue bonds. 
  \item Following Clancy et al. (2016) EAGLE extension, government consumption and investment are productive.
  \item Fiscal instruments are calibrated using exogenous long-run target, and a smoothing parameter. 
  \item The lump-sum taxes are designed to ensure that government debt converges towards a long-run target level.
  \item Central banks follow a Taylor-type interest rate rule.
output growth.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Model calibration}

\begin{frame}{Calibrated variables}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We calibrate the great ratios and fiscal instruments of countries, based on OECD, IMF and EUROSTAT data using long-run averages  over the period of 1995-2020.
  \item Granular calibration allow for heterogeneity of country properties such as govenrmnet spending, and trade openness. 
  \item  Some parameters of the model regarding household, firm, and
government behaviour are kept consistent with the original model, which employs standard values prevelant in the literature
\item To estimate import shares across demand components, we used the methodology of Bussière et al. (2013) to capture both direct and indirect imports using OECD Inter-Country Input-Output (ICIO).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Conclusions}
\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Conclusions and way forward}
\begin{itemize}

    \item When applied to NGEU, the model projects a peak increase in euro area GDP of 0.45\%.
   
    \item NGEU provides substantial stimulus, but the reduction of economic divergences may be constrained by relatively weak spillover effects.
    
    \item This underscores the importance of tailoring resource allocation to country-specific needs and structural features to maximize impact.
    
    \item Future research could extend the framework to include:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Interactions between fiscal and monetary policy, especially at the effective lower
bound.
        \item The role of structural reforms in boosting the efficacy of fiscal stimulus.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
